\noindent\textbf{Scope of improvement.}\quad
\toolname refines a detector with respect to its motivating patch.
A checker may know \emph{where} to warn yet miss \emph{why} the warning should disappear after the fix.
Our results show that refinement also has an earlier task: making the checker recognize the relevant operation in the analyzer's representation.
G19 needs the lowered cleanup callback; G25 needs to look through an expression wrapper.
These are useful repairs, but the remaining scope and path conditions still determine whether a warning is justified.

\noindent\textbf{Evidence as context compression.}\quad
The relevant condition may span source code and analyzer outputs.
Passing the full repository to the model is impractical, while passing only the patch may leave out the representation seen by the checker.
\toolname collects a provenance-tagged summary of patch-relevant facts and makes it available with validation feedback.
The interface follows the general idea of refining an artifact under additional program evidence~\cite{clarke2000cegar,solarlezama2006sketch,nguyen2013semfix,mechtaev2016angelix,long2016prophet}.
Its usefulness varies by case: the no-internal arm also repairs representation problems in G01, G23 and G38, and nearly matches native $F_1$ overall.

\noindent\textbf{Backend complementarity.}\quad
CSA and CodeQL offer different checker formats and evidence interfaces.
The automatic CodeQL case shows that the same editing and paired-validation workflow can operate on a query.
It retains source-derived context and inherited patch-local filters.
The recorded warm execution costs and existing adapter footprint describe that implementation; broader transfer and measured developer effort remain open.

\noindent\textbf{Failure modes.}\quad
Over-pruning is one failure mode, but retaining a warning is not enough either.
In G24, fewer fixed-side reports coexist with surviving reports outside the intended lifecycle.
In G19, a broader cleanup matcher can report at a callee return while the caller's variable is still live.
In G25, a recovered target-line warning can follow an explicitly non-NULL path.
These cases suggest reporting the actual checker edit, the property supported by its diagnostic, and the change in inspection burden together.
This separates successful recognition from the semantic conditions still needing repair.

\noindent\textbf{Generalization.}\quad
The motivating example survives the tested renamings, capacities and equivalent guard forms.
Those tests informed its refinement, and the additional four-mechanism suites are controlled source reductions.
Transfer to unseen projects and vulnerability instances requires a separate evaluation.
