LLM-generated vulnerability checkers can miss a vulnerable revision or warn on both sides of its patch. We present \toolname, an automated refinement workflow that exposes provenance-labelled compiler representations and checker-execution observations, and study how this context changes checker code, warning coverage and report burden. The workflow retains warning recovery or lower fixed-side report counts after normal paired execution; this operational gate does not certify target correctness.

All 39 frozen, generated-only KNighter CSA checkers enter the comparison. With GPT-6-Luna, native \toolname reaches 23 warning-positive vulnerable revisions versus 15 for KNighter's actual refinement loop, with patch-version $F_1=0.575$ versus $0.448$. Native and no-internal refinement have nearly identical $F_1$, while native produces more fixed-side reports. In the complete 15-case noisy stratum, native reduces fixed reports from KNighter's 69 to 44, mainly through one subject. Three paired decodes on 12 subjects show sampling-dependent benefits. An audit of all 11 non-tied comparisons, source-derived perturbations of four mechanisms, and repeated selected-clue interventions distinguish backend-recognition repairs from their remaining semantic obligations. In one checkpoint, target-line recovery repeats, but two of three full-evidence draws report along non-NULL paths. A separate automatic CodeQL example and three-model subset provide bounded feasibility and sensitivity evidence. The results connect useful local repairs, broader matching and invalid diagnoses to concrete code changes; they support neither universal precision gains nor unseen-site generalization.
