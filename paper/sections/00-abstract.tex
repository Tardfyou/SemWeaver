LLM-generated static vulnerability checkers can warn on both vulnerable and fixed code, or remain silent on vulnerable code. We present \toolname, an automated post-synthesis refinement workflow with patch-local, paired validation. Its editor receives provenance-labelled patch context, analyzer outputs, and analyzer-internal evidence: backend-produced representations and checker-execution observations bound to raw output and source scope. It retains warning-recovering or fixed-noise-reducing edits only after normal execution on both revisions.

We evaluate all 39 frozen, generated-only KNighter CSA checkers: 15 initially warn on both revisions and 24 are silent on both. With the same GPT-6-Luna model, \toolname reaches 23 warning-positive vulnerable revisions versus 15 for KNighter's actual refinement loop, with patch-version $F_1=0.575$ versus $0.448$. In the preexisting 15-case fixed-noisy stratum, \toolname retains vulnerable-side warning signals with 44 fixed-side reports versus KNighter's 69; the reduction is concentrated in one subject. Native versus no-internal refinement yields six comparative benefits, five disadvantages and nearly identical aggregate $F_1$; native's additional coverage also increases overall fixed-side noise. Three paired decodes on 12 subjects show sampling-dependent effects. A separate automatic CodeQL example and three-model subset examine feasibility and configuration sensitivity. The results support conditional checker-completion benefits, not independently adjudicated target recall, universally superior precision or unseen-site generalization.
