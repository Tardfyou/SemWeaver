Beyond model variation, we examine source changes in three selected original subjects, G01, G08 and G09; their unchanged-source controls reproduce the original paired counts.

\subsection{Qualitative Findings}
\label{sec:target-results}

To validate whether warning-level improvements reflect genuine semantic understanding, we conduct three supplementary analyses.
First, a target and report audit inspects all 11 non-tied native/no-internal comparisons across 362 report occurrences and 22 code diffs, classifying each report by target association and source support.
Second, mechanism probes construct six controlled variants per case for G12, G19, G24 and G25 (48 files, 96 scans), and a separate intervention replaces native-observation lines with missing markers to test whether evidence removal changes the model's editing decisions.
Third, a controlled source replay rescans three subjects under local renames, inert no-ops and equivalent guard rewrites to measure source-form sensitivity without re-invoking the model.

To explain the native and no-internal differences, we inspect all 11 non-tied comparisons and their reported locations.
The quantitative results match several distinct changes in the checkers.
Among the six native benefits, G12/G19/G25 have bounded source-supported target reports, G09 has conditional support, and G24/G35 retain no supported or conditional target report.
Among the five no-internal advantages, G01/G23/G38 have source support and G05/G14 conditional support.
This source audit explains the warning counts without changing the primary scores.

Both G01 variants repair recognition of the governing loop, but only no-internal checks the preceding capacity guard.
G19 adds a lowered cleanup name.
G23 changes symbol and nested-member lookup; G25 changes sink recognition and destination binding.
These repairs make the intended operation visible to the checker.
Other edits relax predicates: G05 preserves initialization uncertainty, G09 broadens cleanup handling, G12 drops actor restrictions, and G38 combines field-region preservation with a looser usage gate.
G35's new trigger instead reports on an initialized overflow parameter outside the patched target.

The surviving report population matters as much as the count.
G14 chooses different event predicates and retains reports about different lifetime properties.
G24 changes region, NULL and frame tests, removing 25 reports per side; its two survivors concern owner destruction outside the target surviving-owner lifecycle.
Some removed target reports are conditional.
Thus this case shows lower inspection burden, but neither proves a valid target witness was lost nor establishes that the target was preserved.

\subhead{Frozen perturbations.}
On the six positive and six negative fixtures per mechanism, retained native G12 reports at a target location in all positives and no negatives.
This is within its annotation model; the diagnostic's remote-write note points to a local reset.
G19 is silent on its adapted-wrapper suite.
Its separate canonical control produces one supported early-exit report and one wrong-scope allocator-return report, and it misses the renamed wrapper.
G25 reports at target locations in all six positives and five negatives.
The starting and no-internal artifacts are silent for these three cases.
For G24, the starting and no-internal artifacts report at target locations in all six positives and six negatives, with 108/108 total reports.
Native has no frozen target-line hits and 18/12 total reports.
Secondary inspection supports some guard warnings outside that line list; the original location scores remain unchanged.

\subhead{Selected-clue intervention.}
All three full-evidence G25 decisions peel the outer casts around the sink expression; no withheld decision does.
Two full decisions also change bookkeeping.
After the separate compiler-only completion, full recovers target-line presence with negative-side noise, while withheld remains silent (Table~\ref{tab:checkpoint-intervention}).
The full-evidence reports still need inspection.
All 34 reports from repetitions 2/3 follow \texttt{argc<=1}, which selects a concrete non-NULL object.
Repetition 1 has six outer count-access reports that admit a source-feasible NULL completion, without an explicit NULL-return event, alongside unsupported reports.

G19 exposes a different tradeoff.
Full repetitions 1/2 retain name-family tests, while withheld generalizes registration; repetition 3 reverses primary coverage.
The broader matchers can warn on benign cleanup.
Across these artifacts, supported owning-scope exits coexist with reports at callee returns and a write-only cleanup mistaken for a read.

The negative cases are equally important.
Correcting the callback or expression recognized by a checker can activate a useful check, yet leave the object, guard or owning scope wrong.
Even the final source line can contain both a safe pointer-field read and the optional-object dereference of interest.
Conversely, a source-supported guard warning can fall outside the chosen sink-line list.
Examining the edit and its diagnostic together explains why recovery, target-line agreement and semantic support sometimes disagree.
