\toolname combines an auditable post-synthesis checker editor with an empirical account of the benefits and costs of analyzer context. Provenance-labelled compiler/checker evidence and paired validation make the resulting edits and outcomes inspectable. Across 39 generated-only CSA subjects, native GPT reaches 23 warning-positive vulnerable revisions and version-level $F_1=0.575$, versus KNighter's 15 and $0.448$. In the complete 15-case noisy stratum, fixed reports fall from KNighter's 69 to 44 with all vulnerable warning signals retained, primarily through one subject. Overall native noise increases, $F_1$ nearly ties no-internal, and repeated benefits depend on sampling. A separate automatic CodeQL example and three-model subset provide bounded feasibility and sensitivity evidence. The study characterizes conditional checker-completion tradeoffs across warning recovery and report burden; universal precision gains, target-recall proof and unseen-site generalization remain unestablished.
