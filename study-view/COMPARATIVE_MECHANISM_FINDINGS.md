## Supported interpretation and alternatives

The observed repairs illustrate concrete checker-completion problems: executable
API spelling, AST nesting, abstract-region identity, return-frame scope and
overly broad invalidation or trigger predicates. These are implementation-level
observations. It is reasonable to investigate whether native observations make
such mismatches easier to locate, but the recorded contrasts do not prove a
universal effect or that source/output feedback could never reveal them.

The converse is visible: no-internal candidates can recover symbolic-region
handling and guard checks without native records. Broader matching can recover
warning coverage while increasing noise (especially G12). Stopping after three
valid non-improvements can leave an arm behind a better sampled trajectory;
equal maximum reply ceilings do not imply equal actual replies, tokens or work.
Different realized call counts are outcomes or mediators under the shared
stopping policy, not independently assigned confounds by themselves. They do
not invalidate the bounded recorded pipeline contrast; component attribution,
selection/reuse and stochastic uncertainty are separate limitations.
The native-only fixture guard rejects zero main candidates, but its implementation
and overhead are still an asymmetry to disclose.

Repeated E3 strengthens the sampling-sensitivity qualification:36 paired decodes
yield5 benefits,3 comparative disadvantages and28 ties/other outcomes; no subject
benefits comparatively in all three decodes. G19's native15/0 outcome recurs,
