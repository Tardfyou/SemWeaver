\subsection{RQ2: Cross-Backend Feasibility}

We exclude the historical mixed-provenance 20-detector table from automatic-effect counts. In a separate development-only CodeQL replay, an archived automatically generated ImageMagick query starts at $0/0$ vulnerable/fixed rows. Three independent model decodes of the same frozen input reach strict PDS twice ($8/0$ after one call each); the third recovers a warning signal but retains $22/22$ rows after eight calls. The successful edit corrects a relative-path match while retaining exact file, function, and local-variable filters. This establishes patch-local operation on a second detector format, not cross-backend transfer or evidence causality. A second archived query already starts at $2/0$ and is excluded from gain counting. KNighter is not a CodeQL baseline here.

\textbf{Second-backend costs.} On the same existing CodeQL databases, three paired replays per query retain the original $0/0$ and refined $8/0$ outcomes. Median query-plus-decode time is 12.57\,s before and 36.51\,s after refinement (two threads); peak process RSS is 976 and 1,078\,MiB. Successful database-creation logs span approximately 31/30\,s for the two revisions, excluding unlogged startup. The three original model decodes consume 15,122/14,345/110,282 tokens. These are one-case, existing-environment costs, not engineer-hours, cold-install latency, or a general scalability result.

\subsection{RQ3: Internal-Evidence Sensitivity}

All 39 evidence replays completed before treatment: 38 subjects had at least one strictly internal record, and one abstained. Of 192 persisted records, 80 are analyzer-internal, 51 are analyzer outputs, and 61 are source-derived; all 80 internal records pass raw-output and source-scope binding. The full-39 GPT ablation reaches \emph{the same seven PDS cases} and $F_1=0.556$ with or without internal records. Each arm retains 71 fixed-side object alerts under PDS-only adoption. Thus the full-cohort gain cannot be attributed to access to internal records alone.

In the separate per-case-call-matched 15-case condition (Table~\ref{tab:matched14}), the native arm achieves one additional PDS and retains 11 fewer fixed alerts than no-internal. This difference is concentrated in G17, a concurrency checker with 11 starting fixed alerts. It has a different call ceiling and does not overturn the full-cohort tie. On the frozen 12-case repeat subset (five fixed-noisy and seven initially silent cases), native PDS totals are $2,2,3$ and no-internal totals $1,3,2$ (Table~\ref{tab:repeats}). Repeated draws on the same subjects do not establish a stable per-subject evidence effect.

\subsection{Qualitative Findings}

The motivating example preserves the original AST-based traversal and adds an actual-capacity bound, followed by model-produced corrections to guard handling. Development probes vary names, unrelated statements, array capacities, and equivalent control flow. The full checker and failed intermediate candidates remain inspectable. A candidate that silences both revisions is rejected. Compile failures and execution-invalid attempts are not promoted to PDS; the original checker remains the portfolio member when no candidate passes the gate. Qualitative source checks link the five newly warning-positive GPT PDS cases to their patch mechanisms, but do not replace target-level adjudication across the cohort.
