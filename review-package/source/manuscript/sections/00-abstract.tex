LLM-generated static vulnerability checkers can warn on both vulnerable and fixed code, or remain silent on vulnerable code. We study automated post-synthesis refinement under a patch-local, paired-warning gate. \toolname supplies the editor with provenance-labelled patch context, analyzer outputs, and analyzer-internal records: backend-produced intermediate representations bound to raw output and source scope. A candidate is adopted only after independent paired execution confirms a vulnerable-side warning and fixed-side silence.

We evaluate 39 frozen, generated-only KNighter CSA checkers: 15 initially warn on both revisions and 24 are silent on the vulnerable revision. KNighter's actual refinement process yields one patch-discriminating success (PDS) and patch-version $F_1=0.441$; \toolname with the same GPT-6-Luna model yields seven PDS and $F_1=0.556$. In the directly matched 15-case fixed-noisy task, \toolname retains vulnerable-side warning signals and reduces fixed-side object alerts to 60 versus KNighter's 72. Withholding strictly internal records leaves the full-39 outcome unchanged, so this study does not establish a general causal benefit from those records. Runs with GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.3-Flash also improve paired-version discrimination. These results support bounded, automatic reduction of patch-related warning noise and recovery of vulnerable-side warning signals, not independently adjudicated target-vulnerability recall or unseen-site generalization.
