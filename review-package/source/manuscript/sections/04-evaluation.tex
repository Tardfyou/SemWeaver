We evaluate \toolname's patch-related false-positive reduction and its sensitivity to verified analyzer-internal records.  The scope is paired patch revisions and related build objects, not whole-kernel bug discovery.

\subsection{Research Questions}

\textbf{RQ1: Generated CSA checkers.} On the same frozen generated checkers, how do KNighter's actual refinement process and \toolname affect patch discrimination, vulnerable-side hits, and fixed-side noise?

\textbf{RQ2: Cross-backend feasibility and provenance.} Which detector formats can the layer process, and what can the historical independent-generator data support once manual construction and missing lineage are accounted for?

\textbf{RQ3: Evidence sensitivity.} What changes when strictly analyzer-internal records are withheld under an otherwise matched feedback protocol?

\textbf{RQ4: Model robustness.} Do automatic patch-local gains recur with different editor models and across repeated decodes of a frozen subset?

\subsection{Subjects}

E1 freezes one automatically generated CSA checker per selected Linux commit from the upstream KNighter artifacts. The inventory has 286 generated records on 60 commits; 241 records on 39 commits have no upstream refinement-status row. From these, a frozen rule selects one per commit by upstream TP, then TN, then lowest checker index, taking the nonempty repaired checker source. This non-random ranking defines inputs only: its old scores are never used as our outcome labels. We materialize and independently replay all 39 against vulnerable and fixed revisions before treatment. All fail initial PDS: 15 warn on both revisions, while 24 are silent on both. These operational warning strata define different edit tasks, not independently adjudicated target detections or misses. No manually completed checker or human repair enters any effectiveness numerator.

Both KNighter's actual report-driven loop and \toolname start from the same 39 generated checkers. In the 15 fixed-noisy cases, both have a report to refine; this is the direct noise-reduction comparison. In the 24 no-report cases, KNighter's actual branch compiles but makes no model edit, whereas \toolname tries warning recovery. The 39-case comparison is therefore a complete system comparison, not a claim that KNighter unsuccessfully attempted 24 recovery edits. KNighter does not produce or refine CodeQL queries in this cohort.

E2 is a separate automatic CodeQL feasibility replay using an archived query from an independent generator. Historical rows whose construction or lineage was manual, skipped, or ambiguous are excluded from automatic effect counts. CodeQL is not pooled with the 39 CSA cases. E3 compares native and no-internal \toolname arms on all 39 cases with GPT-6-Luna high and an eight-call ceiling; it also repeats a hash-selected subset of five fixed-noisy and seven initially silent cases three times per arm. E4 runs three model configurations on the full 39 under a two-call ceiling and repeats the same 12-case subset. Repeated decodes reuse subjects and are not additional independent checkers.

There is no separate LLM mechanism classifier to score. Fixed rules map patch and analysis facts to prioritized evidence requirements; the editor may request roles in the general workflow. The strict CSA comparison freezes this view and disables such requests. We neither report classifier accuracy nor claim the fixed priorities are optimal. A routing-sensitivity study would hold checker, model, and budget fixed while perturbing visible roles; the historical five-case ablation is not such a study.

\subsection{Metrics}

The primary metric is \emph{patch-discriminating success} (PDS): a detector must emit a warning on the vulnerable revision and none on the fixed revision within the same patch-related scope. This is a version-level warning criterion, not a target-location oracle. We write PDS over a detector $D$, patch $P$, and validation scope $V$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_v(D,P,V) &= \mathbb{1}[\mathrm{alerts}(D,V_{\mathrm{vuln}},P)>0],\\
S_f(D,P,V) &= \mathbb{1}[\mathrm{alerts}(D,V_{\mathrm{fix}},P)=0],\\
\mathrm{PDS}(D,P,V) &= H_v(D,P,V) \land S_f(D,P,V).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pds}
\end{equation}
We report both components: \emph{vulnerable positive} for a warning on the vulnerable side, and \emph{fixed silent} for silence under the same fixed-side scope. A detector that silences both sides loses the vulnerable warning signal and is not a success. Table~\ref{tab:terminology} summarizes the criteria.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \footnotesize
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
  \caption{Evaluation terms and acceptance criteria.}
  \label{tab:terminology}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.17\columnwidth}p{0.43\columnwidth}p{0.12\columnwidth}p{0.18\columnwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    Term & Definition & Success? & Why \\
    \midrule
    Vulnerable positive & checker warns on the vulnerable version & needed & warning signal \\
    Fixed silent & no checker alert in fixed-side scope & not alone & may over-prune \\
    PDS & vulnerable positive $\wedge$ fixed silent & \textbf{yes} & version discrimination \\
    Signal loss & fixed silent but vulnerable warning lost & no & over-pruning \\
    Fixed-noisy & warnings on both versions & no & residual noise \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The refinement gate accepts a candidate $D'$ only when it changes the baseline detector, remains executable for the backend, and satisfies PDS:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Accept}(D',D) ={}& \mathrm{Changed}(D',D)\land \mathrm{AnalyzerOK}(D')\\
&{}\land \mathrm{PDS}(D',P,V).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:accept}
\end{equation}

All conditions use the same PDS definition and track vulnerable-side loss.  E1 counts come from object-level \texttt{scan-build} logs over patch-related build objects.
Static artifact-review findings are retained with each candidate as a separate quality diagnostic. Compilable candidates are replayed on both revisions even when structural review raises a concern; the reported PDS metric is determined by the paired executions. The artifact records both the review and paired verdict.

For the full 39-checker portfolio, we additionally binarize each checker/revision pair by warning presence. Each vulnerable revision is one positive and its fixed revision one negative; thus precision, recall, and $F_1=2TP/(2TP+FP+FN)$ are computed over 78 \emph{patch-version decisions}, not individual analyzer alerts or unseen vulnerability sites. Fixed-side alerts measure review burden, not an exhaustively adjudicated false-positive count. A vulnerable-side warning need not identify the patch's target: G08 warns on allocation failure rather than the registration-failure leak repaired by its patch. The artifact records qualitative diagnostic/patch correspondence, but we do not claim exhaustive target-level recall. Failed analyzer executions are unscored rather than converted into false negatives.

\subsection{Experimental Environment}

CSA runs in a pinned Clang/LLVM 18.1.3 and Python 3.12.3 container with \texttt{scan-build}; the automatic CodeQL replay uses CodeQL 2.26.4 with \texttt{codeql/cpp-all} 8.0.3 and no-build C/C++ databases. Each Linux subject uses its recorded patch commit and parent with \texttt{allyesconfig}, not a common release snapshot. Each starting, baseline-refined, and candidate checker is replayed on the same patch-related build-object scope. We retain frozen manifests, source and checker hashes, raw analyzer outputs, model exchanges, validation logs, and adoption decisions. A provider or execution interruption is not converted into a checker outcome.

\textbf{Use of generative AI in the research workflow.}
LLMs generated the starting checkers and edit candidates; patch-fact extraction, evidence-origin checks, normalization, validation, and adoption are programmatic. An AI coding assistant (Codex) assisted with experiment-driver implementation, audit/analysis scripts, and manuscript drafting. It supplied no ground-truth PDS labels: pinned source, compilation, paired analyzer runs, and the paired-warning rule determine reported outcomes. The artifact retains model settings, English prompts, exchanges, candidates, and script digests; a floating model alias is not an immutable model revision. Authors remain responsible for verifying AI-assisted code and interpretations.

\subsection{Baselines and Variants}

The unmodified checker is the shared before/after reference. E1 runs KNighter's actual output-feedback refinement loop and \toolname from that same checker. The complete 39-case system comparison uses GPT-6-Luna at high reasoning effort. Its direct 15-case fixed-noisy comparison additionally shares the first five frozen fixed-side reports (or all when fewer) and gives each \toolname case at most the number of model calls KNighter actually used for that case; all candidates receive independent paired replay. \toolname may stop earlier. On the 24 no-report cases we compare system outcomes but do not describe KNighter as a recovery-method baseline.

Only the native versus no-internal contrast within \toolname probes access to strictly internal records. The native arm receives hash-bound Clang CFG and call-graph records; the other withholds them but keeps patch, source, analyzer-output feedback, model, budgets, and validator. The all-39 ablation uses eight calls per case; the separate matched 15-case comparison uses KNighter-derived per-case ceilings. Historical five-case, manual, and mixed-provenance contrasts are not evidence of a native-record effect.

E4 uses GPT-6-Luna high, GLM-5.3, and GLM-5.3-Flash to examine whether useful refinements recur across editor models. Each condition retains its named model across all subjects. The full-cohort runs have a two-call ceiling per case. Per-call output ceilings are 16,384 tokens for GPT and 65,536 or 131,072 for GLM; the retained manifests identify each run's ceiling and implementation. All GPT repeats retain two calls per subject. Both additional GLM repeats use the same frozen implementation and a 131,072-token output ceiling, with two calls for fixed-noisy subjects and eight for warning recovery. The first E4 decode reuses the two-call full-cohort run. These are configuration-specific robustness checks, not a budget-matched repeated model ranking; matching calls also does not match token expenditure.

\subsection{Protocol}

E1 counts checker warnings in \texttt{scan-build} outputs on patch-related objects. We run KNighter's report triage, repair, compiler repair, and validation loop, then independently replay its emitted checker. Each \toolname attempt is compiled; compilable candidates are replayed on both patch revisions before feedback informs a later call. Only PDS candidates are adopted. A 39-case portfolio retains the original checker when no candidate passes the gate. Compile failures, validity losses, residual noise, provider interruptions, and paired-execution failures have distinct recorded outcomes. Interrupted suffixes use fresh manifests; completed prefixes remain unduplicated. The artifact includes English templates, frozen inputs, exchanges, raw outputs, provenance bindings, and adoption decisions.
